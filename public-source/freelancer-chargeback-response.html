Useful project records

  • Accepted scope or proposal
  • Invoice and payment record
  • Meeting or attendance log
  • Deliverable and version history
  • Approval, revision, or usage messages

Start with the dispute reason

Do not use the same packet for every chargeback. A service-not-received case needs delivery proof. A not-as-described case needs a comparison between agreed scope and delivered work. A canceled recurring case needs authorization and cancellation timing.

Map every claim to a record

Replace statements such as “the client knew the work was complete” with a dated record: “On Aug 9, the final brief was sent to the email on the paid invoice; the delivery email and access record are Evidence 4.”

A response should remain factual even when the underlying relationship is frustrating.

Common gaps to fix

  • The final scope exists only in scattered chat messages.
  • The invoice description is too vague to identify the deliverable.
  • Files were delivered, but the delivery date is not indexed.
  • Revision requests prove receipt but are buried in a long message export.
  • Refund or cancellation terms were not preserved as shown at purchase.

RebuttalKit cannot manufacture a missing record. It can expose the gap early so you do not accidentally make a claim you cannot support.

Keep future projects easier to defend

  • Confirm scope and delivery method in writing.
  • Use consistent invoice and project identifiers.
  • Preserve acceptance, attendance, and delivery records.
  • Keep refund and cancellation terms versioned.
  • Export important conversations before accounts close.
