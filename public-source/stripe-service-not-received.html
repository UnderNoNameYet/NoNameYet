First records to locate

  1. Dispute notice and deadline
  2. Invoice or transaction record
  3. Accepted proposal or scope
  4. Attendance, completion, or delivery record
  5. Customer communication after delivery

What the response needs to establish

A useful packet normally answers four factual questions: What did the customer purchase? What delivery method and timing were accepted? What record shows performance or delivery? What did the customer do or say afterward?

A long emotional explanation cannot replace those links. Use short statements and make every statement traceable to a record.

Evidence by service type

Freelance or agency project

  • Proposal, statement of work, or accepted email scope
  • Invoice and transaction identifier
  • Deliverable email, file history, project log, or approval
  • Revision requests or customer use after delivery

Coaching, consulting, or appointment

  • Booking confirmation and terms
  • Calendar event, attendance, call log, or session notes
  • Follow-up material sent to the customer
  • Cancellation and refund communication

Remote access or membership

  • Account creation and access details
  • Login, session, or consumption history
  • Welcome and support communication
  • Terms for the billed period

Write the chronology first

Use the transaction, fulfillment, delivery, customer-contact, and dispute dates. If two records conflict, resolve the conflict before writing the response. Do not guess a missing date or imply customer intent.

Before submitting

  • Verify the exact reason and deadline in the live Stripe dashboard.
  • Use file names that match the evidence index.
  • Remove unrelated personal information.
  • Check accepted file formats and size limits.
  • Submit only claims supported by the attached record.
