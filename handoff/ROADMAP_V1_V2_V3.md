### V1.3 commercially ready request flow — gate prepared; real facts blocked

Prepared without opening intake or payment:

- machine-readable 12-gate readiness template
- ignored private operator record
- separate outreach, intake, payment, first-pilot, and launch-experiment results
- non-strict analysis in `npm run quality`
- strict `pilot:strict` and combined `commercial:strict` commands

Three private gates are verified: business contact, security contact, and delivery capacity. Still blocked on real facts needed for the corresponding commercial stage:

- contracting identity/tax details before accepting a contract or payment
- HTTPS intake/deletion route
- launch-state privacy/terms
- processor, payout, and refund position before accepting money
- authenticated approved acquisition channel before individualized outreach

No payment or customer evidence is accepted before these blockers are resolved.

## Deferred workspace concepts

The polished design exploration included Connections, Team, Activity, Plan, and Continuous Verification. These are not V1 routes or operating capabilities.

Reconsider only when repeated paid engagements demonstrate the need and a new security/architecture decision covers:

- credential custody and revocation
- TenantBoundary’s own tenant isolation
- authentication/MFA and role access
- audit logging and incident response
- evidence retention/deletion and data residency
- processor/legal obligations
- billing and cancellation semantics
- secure runner ownership

Continuous Verification remains optional and unpriced until then.

## V2 — repeatable service system

Entry gate: at least three paid engagements with a repeated operational need.

Potential capabilities:

- reusable private adapter and synthetic fixture generator
- richer matrix authoring
- schema 1.1 with code revision, execution time, positive-control, and evidence-manifest fields
- customer-repository regression template
- deterministic private PDF/ZIP delivery
- engagement checklist and deletion automation
- per-engagement encryption/access log
- internal capacity dashboard
- agency/partner package with separate end-customer authorization

Possible public pages: verified trust center, consented case studies, evidence-based FAQ, customer-specific delivery instructions, and an optional local matrix builder.

Still excluded: public arbitrary scanning, automatic policy deployment, and a “secure” certification badge.

## V3 — software only after recurring demand

Entry gate: repeated paid requests for reruns/history plus evidence that software improves margin or trust.

Possible directions:

- scheduled authorized runs against customer-owned staging
- signed execution manifests and change-aware selection
- customer-hosted CI runner and pull-request checks
- isolated organization accounts, report history, controlled sharing, expiry/deletion, audit log, billing, and engagement status
- partner roles with explicit authorization per end customer

Do not incrementally turn the static preview into a credential-handling backend. Write and review a new threat model first.

## Prioritization score

Score 0–2 for paid conversion, risk reduction, delivery time, evidence quality, real buyer request, and maintenance/attack surface. Features below 7/12 normally stay deferred. Credential custody, public scanning, or hosted customer evidence always requires separate architecture/security review.

## Kill criteria

Pause or reposition if ten matched contacts produce no serious conversation after one evidence-based revision, buyers reject the narrow artifact, safe delivery cannot fit price/capacity, legal/insurance needs break pilot economics, or useful evidence requires unacceptable data exposure. Do not treat development effort as market validation.
