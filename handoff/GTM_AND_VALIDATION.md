# Go-to-market and validation

## Goal

Obtain one legitimate paid pilot—or, failing that, two serious scoped buyer conversations—within ten days of public launch.

The experiment tests offer/channel fit. It does not prove a repeatable business after one sale.

## Ideal lead

A founder/technical owner who:

- uses Supabase/Postgres in a multi-tenant app
- is pre-launch or dealing with an authorization concern
- can name roles/tenants/resources
- has staging or a dedicated test project
- is asking for independent verification, not a badge
- has a visible budget and near-term decision

## Priority channels

1. **Recent paid requests** — Upwork or equivalent, through an authenticated operator account and within platform rules.
2. **Explicit public pain** — helpful reply where a founder asks about RLS isolation; disclose affiliation and avoid unsolicited exploit detail.
3. **Warm technical network** — only if an approved business identity exists.
4. **Search-indexed proof assets** — landing page, methodology, fictional sample report.

Do not cold-email from the private Outlook address. Do not spam GitHub issues, scraped emails, or unrelated communities.

## Current demand evidence

Recent public requests include:

- independent Supabase RLS/authorization review around $300
- Lovable/Supabase security audit around $500
- pre-launch Supabase/Postgres security review around $700
- urgent cross-tenant data-leak repair
- multi-tenant education SaaS role/isolation audit
- manual SaaS authorization testing beyond automated scans

These validate the problem and price neighborhood. They do not mean the jobs remain open or that TenantProof will win.

## Positioning

### One line

TenantProof executes the tenant and role matrix your RLS badge cannot prove, then hands you redacted before/after evidence.

### What makes it different

- application semantics agreed before testing
- negative and positive controls
- table, function, and storage boundaries
- failure-to-remediation-to-retest continuity
- uncertainty preserved
- non-sensitive request path
- fixed entry scope

### Do not lead with

- AI
- number of tools
- generic “cybersecurity”
- fear statistics
- design quality
- compliance
- “100% isolation”

## Proposal framework

1. Mirror the buyer’s exact boundary.
2. Explain the first three execution steps.
3. Name the deliverable.
4. State relevant limits and staging preference.
5. Ask one qualifying question.
6. Link fictional sample and label it.

### Example — independent RLS review

> I would turn your current role/tenant expectations into a small allow/deny matrix, execute each path with synthetic identities in staging, and return a redacted pass/fail report with the exact unresolved areas. I would not treat “RLS enabled” as proof or promise the entire app is secure. For your described scope, the $349 Boundary Verification package is the likely starting point. Do you already have two test tenants and representative role accounts in staging?

### Example — broader pre-launch audit

> Your request is broader than TenantProof’s fixed boundary package, so I would start by isolating the multi-tenant authorization work: tables, role paths, selected RPCs, and storage. The output is an expectation matrix, executed evidence, remediation, and retest—not an automated score. After that boundary is scoped, we can identify what remains for a full application-security review rather than mislabeling one as the other.

Never claim prior customers, references, or portfolio work. Use the explicitly fictional sample to demonstrate output structure.

## Ten-day sequence

### Day 0 — readiness

- live proof pages and intake
- validated sample/report
- proposal assets
- capacity reserved for one pilot
- payment/legal path ready before accepting funds

### Days 1–2 — top three applications

Submit only to the three strongest current paid requests. Personalize the first paragraph and scope. Record competition/proposal activity if visible.

### Days 3–4 — follow-up and public help

- one concise follow-up where platform permits
- two high-value replies to explicit public questions
- no links unless relevant and allowed
- record objections

### Days 5–6 — iterate once

Change one variable from evidence:

- headline
- package scope
- deliverable preview
- qualification friction
- channel

Do not rebuild the product.

### Days 7–8 — remaining matched leads

Contact up to seven additional leads, keeping total at ten.

### Days 9–10 — decision

- paid pilot: deliver and record unit economics
- serious conversations/no sale: identify blocker and run one focused revision
- no serious conversation: stop active outreach and revise offer/channel/proof

## Objection handling

### “I can ask AI to write policies.”

Agree. TenantProof is not selling policy text; it sells agreed execution, before/after evidence, and regression checks against application-specific roles.

### “A scanner says RLS is enabled.”

Explain that configuration presence and business authorization behavior are different questions. Show matrix/evidence structure.

### “Why not a broad pentest?”

A broad pentest may be appropriate. TenantProof is a narrower, faster decision artifact for the tenant boundary and should not be misrepresented as a substitute.

### “Can you guarantee no cross-tenant leak?”

No. Offer scoped contextual evidence and explicit residual scope.

### “Can I send production credentials now?”

No. Qualify and authorize first; arrange least-privileged secure exchange.

## Content strategy

Publish only decision-useful assets:

- fictional report anatomy
- how to define a tenant-boundary matrix
- why `WITH CHECK` matters for proposed rows
- function/storage boundary consistency
- what a pass does and does not mean
- staging fixture design

Avoid sensational breach content, copied statistics, or generic SEO filler.

## Stop rules

- maximum initial contacts: ten
- no bulk automation
- no scraped personal email
- no fake case studies
- no blind discounting
- no feature expansion to avoid selling
- no active customer work without authorization and capacity
