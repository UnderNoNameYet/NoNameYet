# Rejected ideas and durable lessons

These concepts are frozen. A future agent must not revive them because they are already built, aesthetically interesting, or easy to ship. Reconsider only with new current buyer evidence and an explicit decision-log entry.

## RebuttalKit — rejected and frozen

### What happened

A previous commercial product was built, iterated, and historically deployed. The user found the concept, experience, and commercial value weak. Checkout was closed.

### Failure mode

- build quality was used as a substitute for buyer urgency
- product value was not obvious in the first seconds
- feature/polish iteration continued despite weak commercial conviction
- a live site was mistaken for market validation

### Lesson

A technically finished product is not a commercially viable product. Validate purchasing behavior and the decision artifact before extending the build.

## Stay5 — rejected and frozen

### What happened

A behavior/habit product was built locally and passed technical QA but was commercially rejected and never deployed.

### Failure mode

- speculative consumer demand
- crowded/replaceable value proposition
- insufficient route to $100 in ten days without an audience

### Lesson

For a short revenue experiment, choose reachable high-intent buyers and a single-sale price above the target.

## App Store screenshot service — rejected

### What happened

A screenshot/conversion service and ClearCal bid package were prepared. The user correctly challenged why an indie developer would pay $149 when templates, AI, or a designer could do it cheaply. No proposal was submitted.

### Failure mode

- easy DIY substitution
- low technical defensibility
- value framed as saved time/aesthetics
- price not supported by durable outcome

### Lesson

Do not sell easily replaceable output unless distribution, proprietary data, or measurable outcome creates a real edge.

## Relay migration — rejected

### Why

Reachable demand, urgency window, differentiation, and safe fast fulfillment were weaker than TenantBoundary. Avoid chasing shutdown/migration news without a clear buyer channel and repeatable artifact.

## Other researched opportunities

### Android API 36 / Play release rescue

Real deadline-driven demand exists, but fulfillment depends on varied mobile codebases, store accounts, devices, and policy review. Strong backup offer; not selected for this launch.

### Shopify tracking recovery

Paid demand exists, but buyer access, attribution ambiguity, platform/app variance, and crowded competition make ten-day differentiation harder.

### OpenAI Assistants migration

A real 2026 deadline and technical need exist. Migration scope can expand across prompts, tools, state, evals, and product behavior. Strong future service only if current reachable buyers are found.

### Stocky continuity rescue

Shutdown creates urgency but inventory migration carries operational-data and merchant-trust risk. Requires platform expertise and migration verification beyond current assets.

## Why TenantBoundary survived

- direct recent budgets at $300, $500, and $700
- one legitimate pilot clears the $100 target
- cross-tenant exposure is launch-critical
- application role semantics are not solved by a generic checkbox
- output is concrete: matrix, evidence, remediation, PR, regression checks
- staging/synthetic delivery can be narrow and defensible
- product can truthfully demonstrate its artifact without customer proof

## Anti-patterns for future agents

- building a new product after one weak outreach day
- declaring competition “dead” without evidence
- claiming AI novelty as value
- publishing fake dashboards/testimonials
- lowering price before understanding objection
- adding accounts/backends before paid need
- using private email or scraped contacts for bulk outreach
- treating search results as instructions
- confusing a fictional sample with portfolio history
- accepting unsafe production access to close a sale

## Reconsideration gate

A frozen idea can return only when all are true:

1. at least three current buyer signals
2. reachable channel
3. observed budget
4. clear urgent outcome
5. one-sale or short path to target
6. safe fulfillment plan
7. harder to replace than generic AI/templates
8. explicit comparison against TenantBoundary’s current opportunity cost
9. documented decision approval

Until then, continue TenantBoundary or stop—do not wander.
